What we build

What the engine does

It is set up from your forms, clause library, executed deals and house rules.

Answers across everything you have issued

Search forms, wordings, endorsements, slips and executed deals together. You get the passage and the document it sits in.

Read with its endorsements

A policy is read with the endorsements that amend it, so the answer reflects the wording in force.

Quoted from the source

Every passage in an answer is found in one of your documents. Anything that cannot be found is flagged.

Scans go to your team

If a page has no readable text, it comes back to you unanswered.

It remembers, and learns from your team

What it learns is kept as rules and records in your own system. None of it trains a model.

Documents tied to their deals

Which endorsement amends which policy, which deal each document belongs to, and who the parties are. Worked out once and kept.

Decisions carry forward

When a reviewer approves or corrects a clause, the next draft follows that choice. Your team makes each call once.

Current as the library grows

New and revised documents are picked up as they are saved to your document system.

Drafts from your own precedents

Give it the deal inputs and the precedent to start from.

Your rules first

Where your forms and rules cover a point, the draft follows them. Suggested language is labeled until your team adopts it.

One deal, each entity's forms

The same deal can be drafted on the forms of each carrier entity or market you write through.

Your reviewer signs off

Drafts stay drafts until a reviewer approves them. The engine does not issue, bind or send anything.

A record that holds up later

Wordings get questioned years after they were issued, often after the form has changed.

Answers keep their version

An answer points at the document as it read that day. Later edits to the form do not change it.

A name on every answer

Who asked, who approved it, and when.

Copies kept past your file history

Some storage plans keep thirty days of file history. The page you relied on is kept anyway.

Questions

What insurers ask first

Which lines of business is this for?

Any line where the paper is written for the deal: specialty and financial lines, manuscript wordings, program business, binding authorities and reinsurance treaties. If your documents are mostly standard forms, a shared product will usually serve you better, and we will say so on the first call.

Does the engine make underwriting or claims decisions?

No. It locates, drafts and flags. Underwriting, pricing, claims and the decision to issue anything stay with your people.

What will our vendor review ask that you cannot answer yet?

A SOC 2 report or ISO certificate, a third-party penetration test, hosting inside your own cloud account, and keys you hold yourself. We have none of those today. The security page keeps that list current.

We have our own engineers. Should we build this ourselves?

Sometimes that is the right call. If you work with us, what the system learns from your documents belongs to you, and the terms for taking it with you are written into the agreement before the build starts. Today we run each system on our own infrastructure. Hosting it inside your own cloud account is not built yet. Build or buy, in more detail.

Who owns what gets built?

What the system learns from your documents belongs to you. The terms for taking it with you are written into the agreement before the build starts.

What does it cost?

The one-week prototype is at no charge. The build is a fixed price, quoted before work starts. Builds start at $25,000. The monthly fee starts at $5,500 and covers hosting, support and every model provider cost. Nothing is passed through. The engagement model.

Yes. Book a demo, sign the NDA, and within the first week there is a working prototype running on your own wordings.
