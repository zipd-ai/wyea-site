WYEA / What we build / Discovery and review

What we build · 02

Production sets read against the requests actually served

A production set is indexed, ranked against the requests your firm was served, and handed back as a review queue. Privilege candidates are surfaced with the reason and held for an attorney to decide.

Book a 30-minute call See all four systems

How it is built

From a delivery of files to a queue a reviewer can work

The volume problem is a reading problem. That part is handled first.

What comes in, and what survives it

A delivery arrives as native files, scans, and mail containers. Everything that identifies a document has to come through intact.

Text extraction

Native files are opened and read; scanned pages go through recognition so they are searchable alongside everything else.

Bates and custodians preserved

Ranges, custodians, and dates are read from the delivery and carried through to the log untouched.

Families kept together

Attachments stay with their parent message, so a document is never reviewed without the cover it arrived under.

How a document is ranked

Relevance here means relevance to the requests your firm was actually served, not to a general notion of the case.

Against the requests served

Your served requests are entered once. Every document in the set is scored against them individually.

With the request number shown

A hit says which request it answers, so a reviewer can agree or disagree with the reason rather than the score.

With the passage in view

The text that triggered the hit is shown beside the call, so nothing has to be taken on trust.

How review runs

A queue, a record, and a log you can produce

  1. 01

    Set is loaded

    Bates ranges, custodians, and dates are read from the delivery and kept intact.

  2. 02

    Requests are mapped

    Your served requests are entered once, and every document is scored against them.

  3. 03

    Reviewers work the queue

    Calls are made by your team, with the reasoning and the source page in view.

  4. 04

    Log is produced

    The privilege log and production record come out of the same record the review ran on.

Worked examples

What firms put through it

The same queue, pointed at different problems.

An incoming production from the other side

Tens of thousands of pages arrive on a deadline. The set is indexed overnight and comes back ranked against your own requests, so the first day of review starts with the documents that answer what you asked for.

Your own client's collection

Before anything is produced, the collection is read against the requests served on your client, with candidate privilege documents pulled aside for an attorney and everything else grouped by request.

Privilege log preparation

The log is generated from the same record the review ran on, so the entries match the calls that were actually made, with the reviewer and the date attached to each one.

Deposition exhibit assembly

Documents tied to a custodian or a date range are pulled into an exhibit set with Bates numbers intact and a cover index, ready for the reporter.

A second look at an old production

A set produced years ago is re-indexed against a new theory of the case, so the documents that matter now surface without re-reading the whole delivery.

What stays with your attorneys

Nothing is produced or withheld by software

The system narrows the reading. Every decision that carries a consequence is made by a person at your firm.
