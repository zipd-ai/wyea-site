What we build · 03

Deadlines computed from the rules, shown with the authority

Dates are calculated from the service method, the court, and the rules that govern the matter, then calendared with the citation attached, so the attorney who signs off can check the arithmetic instead of taking it on faith.

How it is built

The clerical layer of a matter, done the same way every time

Intake and calendaring are where quiet mistakes get made. They are also the most mechanical part of the work.

What opens a matter

Everything the firm needs to know before the file exists is pulled from the opening documents rather than retyped.

Parties and counsel

Names, roles, and counsel of record read from the complaint or the engagement papers, with the caption assembled from them.

Conflict prompts

Parties and related entities checked against your existing matters, with anything close raised before the file opens.

File structure

The matter is laid out the way your firm already keeps files, so nobody has to learn a new place to put things.

What a computed date carries

A date without its reasoning is a liability. Every deadline on the calendar shows the three things behind it.

The trigger

What was served or filed, on what date, and by what method, recorded as the event the count runs from.

The rule

The governing statute, rule of court, or local rule for that trigger in that court, cited on the entry.

The arithmetic

The days added, the holidays and weekends skipped, and the extension applied for the service method, shown in full.

How a date is produced

Four inputs, and a citation on the output

  1. 01

    Trigger is recorded

    What was served or filed, on what date, and by what method.

  2. 02

    Rule is selected

    The governing statute or rule for that trigger, in that court.

  3. 03

    Days are counted

    Court holidays, weekends, and extensions for the service method applied.

  4. 04

    Date is calendared

    Written to the calendar with the rule cited and the calculation shown.

A computed date is a starting point for the responsible attorney, not a substitute for one. Every calculation is shown so it can be checked, and the calendar records who confirmed it.

Worked examples

What firms put on it

Court dates and firm dates on one calendar, each with its source.

Discovery response deadlines

Service of requests is docketed with its method, the response date is computed under the governing section, and your firm's own collection and draft deadlines are placed ahead of it so the work starts on time rather than on the due date.

Motion and opposition schedules

A hearing date sets the filing, opposition, and reply dates backwards from it, with the court's own local rules applied and each entry citing the rule it came from.

Trial-setting cascades

One trial date drives the expert, discovery cutoff, and motion-in-limine dates together, and moving the trial date moves the whole set rather than leaving stale entries behind.

Statute of limitations watch

Accrual dates captured at intake are tracked with the applicable period and tolling noted, and surfaced well before they matter rather than in the month they expire.

Your firm's own lead times

The internal rules a firm actually runs on, collection complete fourteen days out, draft circulated seven days out, partner review three days out, sit on the same calendar as the court dates.
