Questions

What insurers ask first

What stops it making something up?

A passage has to be in one of your documents before it is written down as an answer. If it is not there, you see it flagged. If a document could not be read at all, a scanned endorsement for instance, it goes on your list instead of being filled in from context. How the check works.

Which systems does it read from?

Your document system and your mail. Dropbox is connected first; SharePoint, OneDrive, Google Drive, iManage and NetDocuments are what the connecting work was written for. Your document system stays the system of record, and we keep no competing library. Connected sources.

What happens when someone edits the source document?

The answer you already have stays as it was. It points at the document as it read on the day you got it, so an edit to the form in June leaves March's answer exactly as it was given, and you can still open what you relied on. How that works.

Does it make underwriting or claims decisions?

No. It finds, drafts and flags. Underwriting, pricing, claims and the decision to issue, bind or send anything stay with your people. Drafts stay drafts until a reviewer approves them.

Is our content mixed with another insurer's?

No. Each insurer runs in its own system, with its own database, network and sign-in. Nothing is retrieved across clients and nothing you put in trains a model. Security.

Should we buy a shared product instead?

If your documents are mostly standard forms, a shared product will usually serve you better, and we will say so on the first call. A firm-owned engine earns its cost when your wordings are manuscript, your programs run across several carriers, or the same deal is papered on several entities' forms.
