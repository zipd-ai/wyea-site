The grounding rule

Three answers, and one of them is "I could not check".

Most systems have two states and quietly collapse the third into a confident sentence. This one keeps them apart.

Located

The quote was found in the source, character for character, after whitespace is normalised.

It carries its span

The answer records the character offsets where the text was found, so you can open the document and land on it.

It carries its version

The offsets mean nothing without the version they refer to, so the version identity travels with them.

It survives the file changing

A later edit does not silently move the answer. It creates a new record and leaves the old one resolvable.

Not located

The quote was looked for and was not there. This is the case that produces hallucinations elsewhere.

It is flagged, not dropped

The claim stays visible with a flag on it. Deleting it quietly would hide that the system tried and failed.

It is never rephrased into safety

There is no fallback that softens an unlocatable quote into a vaguer sentence that no longer needs a source.

The run records it

Whoever reviews the work can see how many claims failed the check, not just the ones that passed.

Not checked

The source could not be read at all. Treating this as a failed check would be as wrong as treating it as a pass.

Permissions can change mid-run

A document you could read on Monday may be walled on Tuesday. The system says so rather than answering from memory.

Version history expires

Providers age out old versions on a schedule set by the plan you pay for, which is why the evidentiary cache exists.

A limitation is a result

When a connector cannot supply a real version identity it says so, rather than substituting a timestamp and moving on.

Questions

The details, before you have to ask

What stops it making something up?

A claim only stands if its quote is located in the source document, character for character. If the quote is not there, the claim is flagged instead of asserted. If the source could not be read at all, that is recorded as not checked, which is a different state from false. How the check works.

Which systems does it read from?

Your document store, your mail, and the public court record. Dropbox is the first connector; SharePoint, OneDrive, Google Drive, iManage and NetDocuments are what the connector layer is designed against. Your store stays the system of record and we keep no competing library. The connector list.

What happens when someone edits the source document?

Every answer binds to one version identity supplied by your own platform, never to whatever the file says now. An edit writes a new evidence record rather than changing the old one, so an answer given in March still resolves against the March version. How evidence is recorded.

Is our content mixed with another firm's?

No. Each firm runs in its own cell with its own database, network and identity. There is no shared content plane, no cross-firm retrieval, and no training on firm content.

Do you only work with law firms?

No. The system is built around documents, versions and evidence rather than around one profession. Legal work is where it started. Insurance is the other line it runs in.

What does an engagement cost?

It starts with a fixed-price prototype running on your own documents, quoted before we begin. If that earns the rest, the build is scoped the same way. The engagement model.
