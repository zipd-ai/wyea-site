Why it is built one firm at a time

Your firm has an edge. So should your software.

Software sold to a whole market has to behave the same way for everyone who buys it. That is what makes it cheap, and for plenty of work it is the right thing to buy.

Your conventions are the configuration

The way you open a matter, the format your partners insist on, the checks you run before anything goes out. On a shared product those are settings you bend to fit. Here they are what the system is shaped from.

What you know stays in the system

The courts you appear in, the officers you use, the parties across your matters. Reviewed once with you, then held. Nobody retypes them and nobody guesses at them later.

Changes do not queue behind a roadmap

When the work changes, two engineers who already know your practice change the software. There is no ticket sitting behind three hundred other firms asking for the opposite thing.

Context

Context does not get lost.

Nobody re-explains the file, and nothing you were told six months ago quietly changes underneath you.

It already knows the file

Who the parties are, which court or which policy, what is due and when. Set up once with your team and used by everything after.

Nobody is asked twice

Deadlines, documents and mail all belong to the same file, so they do not drift out of step with each other.

It uses the lists your firm already vetted

Courts, officers, counterparties. Confirmed with your people at the start and then left alone. Nothing is looked up fresh each time and assumed correct.

A new person starts where the last one stopped

Whoever picks the file up reads what is already on it. The story does not have to be rebuilt out of somebody's sent folder.

What you said in March still stands up in September

Advice gets questioned long after the document behind it has moved on. Your answer keeps pointing at that document as it read that day.

An edit does not change what you were told

Somebody revising the contract in June leaves March's answer exactly as it was given.

It remembers for longer than Dropbox does

Some Dropbox plans keep thirty days of file history. A matter runs for years, so a copy of the page you relied on is kept regardless.

Every answer has a name against it

Who asked, who approved it, and when. A question about a piece of work has somewhere to go.

The details, before you have to ask

What stops it making something up?

A passage has to actually be in one of your documents before it is written down as an answer. If it is not there, you see it flagged rather than stated. If a document could not be read at all, a scanned exhibit for instance, that goes on your list instead of being filled in from context. How the check works.

Which systems does it read from?

Your document store, your mail, and the public court record. Dropbox is the first connector; SharePoint, OneDrive, Google Drive, iManage and NetDocuments are what the connector layer is designed against. Your store stays the system of record and we keep no competing library. The connector list.

What happens when someone edits the source document?

Nothing. An answer points at the document as it read on the day you got the answer, not at whatever it says now. Somebody editing the contract in June does not quietly change what you were told in March, and in September you can still open exactly what you relied on. How that works.

Why not just buy a shared platform?

For a lot of firms that is the right call, and we will say so on the first call if it is. A shared product has to work the same way for everyone who buys it, which is what makes it affordable. The trade is that the parts of your practice that are actually yours become settings you work around, and a change you need waits behind everyone else's. If the work you want handled is the work that makes you different, that trade stops being worth it. The three paths, compared.

Is our content mixed with another firm's?

No. Each firm runs in its own system, with its own database, network and sign-in. There is no shared content plane, no cross-firm retrieval, and no training on firm content.

Do you only work with law firms?

No. The system is built around documents, versions and evidence rather than around one profession. Legal work is where it started. Insurance is the other line it runs in.

What does an engagement cost?

It starts with a fixed-price prototype running on your own documents, quoted before we begin. If that earns the rest, the build is scoped the same way. The engagement model.
