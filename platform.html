Platform

A document engine that can show you where every answer came from

The system reads the documents your company already keeps, answers questions against them, and binds each answer to the exact version of the exact file it was found in. Where it cannot find something, it says so.

The shape of it

Four parts

Each one exists because of a specific way document systems behave.

Connected sources

Your document system and your Outlook mail. Your document system stays the system of record.

Your store stays the record

The platform does not become a second library your people have to keep in sync. Your document system remains the thing that is true.

Providers differ, and it matters

Version durability, digest algorithm, change-feed shape and access control all vary. The connector declares what it can actually promise.

Filenames are treated as sensitive

At an insurer the filename is often the sensitive part, so it is never used to decide what something is or who may see it.

Grounding and verification

A quote is located in the source or it does not become an answer.

It is there, or it is flagged

A passage has to be in one of your documents before it is written as an answer. A passage that is not found is flagged for you.

No confidence score

There is no number between right and wrong for somebody to read generously at the end of the day.

Unreadable is its own answer

If the source could not be opened, the result is not checked. Checked and false is a separate result.

Evidence and the record

An answer points at the document as your own system had it that day.

The proof is your own system's

What an answer points at is your own system's record of that document.

Old answers are never rewritten

When a document changes, the answer you were already given keeps pointing at what it was actually based on.

It outlives your provider's memory

Dropbox and the rest drop old versions on a schedule of their own. Work you have already relied on stays openable anyway.

Isolation

One client, one system, with nothing shared underneath.

Own database, network, identity

Each insurer's system has its own database, so no shared table holds two insurers' rows.

Nothing pooled

No shared pile for anyone else to search, no reaching across insurers, and nothing of yours trains a model. Instances share software and no documents.

We publish the limits

We run it for you. The security page says plainly what that does and does not mean today.

Where it is used

Specialty insurance

Wordings, endorsements, slips, binding authority agreements and treaties. The question is usually what a specific document says, as amended, on the paper of a specific entity.
