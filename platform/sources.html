Platform

It reads your systems. It does not replace them.

Documents stay where your people already put them. The platform connects to the document system, reads versions from it, and writes back only where you turn that on. Nothing about your filing has to change first.

Why each system has its own connector

Providers vary in ways you cannot paper over

A lowest common denominator port would either promise things a provider cannot do, or throw away the guarantees a good one gives you.

What actually differs

Four axes, and each of them changes what the platform can honestly say about a document it just read.

How long a version lasts

Dropbox keeps history for 30 days on some plans and ten years on others, and upgrading is not retroactive.

Whether it can prove a file is unchanged

Some platforms can. Others cannot, and we tell you which yours is.

Whether the system knows your deals

Some do. Most do not, and the mapping from folders to programs and accounts becomes configuration for each insurer.

What the platform refuses to do

The failure mode here is quiet, and it invalidates evidence in bulk, many documents at once.

Pass off a guess as a record

If your platform cannot say which version was read, we tell you that. We do not quietly use the file date.

Bind a claim to current

A request can ask for the current file, but the exact version is settled before anything is read.

Treat a rename as a change

Rename and move alter neither the object id nor the version, so they do not disturb evidence already recorded.
